Every trading strategy that gets sold comes with a backtest. Almost none come with the number that determines whether that backtest meant anything. We measured it — across 18 assets and 576 parameter sets — and the result should change how you read every performance chart you see.

What we did

For each of 18 assets we tested five rule-based strategies — Buy & Hold, a 50/200 moving-average crossover, RSI(2) mean reversion, 12-month time-series momentum, and volatility targeting. Then, separately, we fitted 576 moving-average parameter combinations on the first 70% of each asset's history and graded every one on the final 30% it had never seen. Costs of 5bps per position change are applied throughout, and every signal is shifted one day so nothing trades on information it would not have had.

The finding

Across all 18 assets, the mean correlation between in-sample and out-of-sample performance was -0.040 (median -0.014, range -0.362 to +0.311). In plain terms: a parameter set that looked excellent while you tuned it carried essentially no information about how it would behave on data it had never seen.

This is the mechanism behind almost every “proven system” that fails in live trading. Optimisation gives a strategy more freedom to memorise the past. The parameter set that fits history best is often the one most precisely fitted to noise.

What actually helped

Volatility targeting cut drawdown by an average of 35.1 percentage points across the 18 assets where we measured it — and it never delivered the highest return. That is the real trade: you give up headline return to buy survivability. Volatility targeting had the lowest mean return of all five strategies (7.6% versus 17.8% for buy and hold) and the lowest mean Sharpe. What it bought was the ability to stay in the position at all.

Common questions

Does optimising a strategy improve out-of-sample results?

Not in this data. Mean in-sample/out-of-sample correlation: -0.040. Tuning selected noise.

How many parameter sets?

576, each fitted on 70% of history and graded on the unseen 30%.

What should I look at instead of returns?

Sharpe and Calmar. Mean Sharpe across assets: Buy & Hold 0.64, volatility targeting 0.62, momentum 0.54.
