Why backtests fail: the out-of-sample evidence

Original backtest research · 18 assets · 576 parameter sets · 2005–2026

So why do so many backtests fail in live trading? The answer is measurable. Across 18 assets and 576 moving-average parameter sets, the mean in-sample versus out-of-sample correlation was -0.040.

The mechanism

Optimisation gives a strategy more freedom to memorise the past. The parameter set that fits history best is frequently the one most precisely fitted to noise rather than to a persistent effect. When the strategy meets genuinely new data, there is nothing left to hold it up.

The measurement. Across 18 assets and 576 moving-average parameter sets, the mean in-sample versus out-of-sample correlation was -0.040 (median -0.014). In sample, the best parameter set on Bitcoin returned 56.7%/yr; on unseen data the same rules returned 20.9%/yr. On a large-cap tech basket, 25.8%/yr collapsed to -1.2%.

The three usual culprits

  1. Optimisation. Tuning parameters until the curve looks good, then treating that curve as evidence.
  2. Lookahead. Using information that would not have been available at the moment of the trade.
  3. Ignoring costs. Free trading is a fantasy; 5bps per position change materially changes high-turnover rules.

What to do instead

Fit on a portion of history, then grade on the remainder you have never looked at. Compute the in-sample versus out-of-sample correlation across your whole sweep, not just for the winner. Compare Sharpe and Calmar rather than raw return. And add realistic costs.
