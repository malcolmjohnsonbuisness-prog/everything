Volatility targeting explained: the one rule that cut drawdown

Original backtest research · 18 assets · 576 parameter sets · 2005–2026

Volatility targeting scales exposure inversely to recent volatility. Across our panel it reduced average maximum drawdown by 35.1 percentage points, at the cost of lower returns.

How it works

Exposure is scaled inversely to recent realised volatility. When the market is calm you hold more; when it is violent you hold less. The aim is not higher returns but a more stable ride.

Our measurement. Across the 18 assets where we measured it, volatility targeting cut average maximum drawdown by 35.1 percentage points versus buy and hold, and recorded the best mean Calmar of any strategy tested (0.32).
What it costs. It had the lowest mean CAGR of the five strategies (7.6%). It buys survivability, not returns.
