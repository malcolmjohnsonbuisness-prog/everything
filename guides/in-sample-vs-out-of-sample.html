In-sample vs out-of-sample: the number that exposes optimisation

Original backtest research · 18 assets · 576 parameter sets · 2005–2026

In-sample versus out-of-sample correlation measures whether tuning a strategy actually predicts future performance. Across 18 assets and 576 parameter sets, ours measured -0.040.

Definitions

In-sample is the data you used to choose the rules. Out-of-sample is data the rules have never seen. Performance on the second is the only meaningful test of a strategy — and almost nobody publishes the relationship between them.

Our measurement across 18 assets: mean correlation -0.040, median -0.014, range -0.362 to +0.311. 576 parameter sets, each fitted on 70% of history and graded on the unseen 30%.

How to compute it

Run your parameter sweep on the in-sample period, record each set's performance, then run the identical sets on the out-of-sample period. Correlate the two lists. Near zero means your optimisation carried no information.
