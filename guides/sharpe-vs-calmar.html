Sharpe vs Calmar: which risk-adjusted metric to trust

Original backtest research · 18 assets · 576 parameter sets · 2005–2026

Sharpe divides return by volatility; Calmar divides return by maximum drawdown. Across 18 assets, volatility targeting had the best mean Calmar while buy and hold had the better Sharpe.

The difference

Sharpe = excess return divided by volatility. Calmar = annual return divided by maximum drawdown. Sharpe penalises all movement; Calmar penalises only the worst fall.

Why it matters

A strategy can have a respectable Sharpe and still contain a drawdown deep enough that nobody would sit through it. Calmar captures that. Across our panel, buy and hold averaged a Sharpe of 0.64 but a mean maximum drawdown of -63.8%, while volatility targeting had a lower Sharpe (0.62) but the best mean Calmar (0.32).

Use both

Sharpe tells you about the general smoothness of returns. Calmar tells you whether the strategy is survivable. A strategy you abandon at the bottom hands you neither.
